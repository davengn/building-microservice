## Why this lesson matters

Real business processes often cross multiple service boundaries. This lesson focuses on making that progress visible, recoverable, and owned.

- Distributed workflows need explicit failure stories.
- Compensation is not the same as a database rollback.
- Process visibility matters for support and operations.

## Core study path

Study the workflow choices by asking where state lives and who is responsible for moving the process forward.

- Use orchestration when a clear process owner improves visibility.
- Use choreography when services can react to facts independently.
- Make failure and compensation paths first-class design work.

## Practice cues

Model a checkout or onboarding flow. For each step, note the owning service, the event or command, the timeout behavior, and the compensation path.

- Avoid pretending cross-service work is a single transaction.
- Identify the signal that tells support what happened.
- Decide whether central coordination is helping or hiding coupling.
