## Why this lesson matters

This lesson anchors the rest of the site. A microservice is not simply a small web service; it is a deployable unit that lets a team change part of a system with fewer coordination costs.

- Look for the link between service boundaries and team ownership.
- Notice that independent deployability creates new operational duties.
- Keep asking whether distribution is solving a real coordination problem.

## Core study path

Read the chapter as a vocabulary reset. Focus on autonomy, coupling, bounded business capability, and the difference between a service landscape and a collection of endpoints.

- Start with the goal: independent change.
- Then study the cost: remote calls, versioning, deployment, and failure handling.
- Finish by comparing microservices with modular monolith design.

## Practice cues

Use this chapter to create a decision filter. If you cannot name the business capability, owning team, data boundary, and operational owner, the service boundary is probably still unclear.

- Write one sentence describing the service's reason to exist.
- List the dependencies that would stop it from changing independently.
- Name the operational signals you would need on day one.
